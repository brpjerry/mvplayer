MV Player import rules: the judge

How a track comes to have a music video when a model is asked instead of the rules of thumb. Two things the judge can do, chosen in Settings → Judge. In judge mode it runs the lookup: it writes the search, picks what is listened to and gives the verdict on every upload; what the main page drops on sight, whose channel an upload is on, version and fan words, small channels and the score no longer apply, and what stays are the audio check, the length check and your verdict on what the judge is not sure of. In advice mode nothing in the lookup changes: each card under review shows what the judge would do and how sure it is. Rules of the main page are cited by their ids (R0, G1); this page's own are Q1, S2 …

A new page, proposed: not approved yet, and nothing in the code implements it. The words the judge is given (K) were refined against 199 of your own verdicts and measured on three models (M); the numbers under N follow from that.

The two modes

Judge modeThe judge runs the lookup

Q → S → L → V → W, then your verdict (X) on what it leaves. Of the main page, A1 and A6–A11 (when a track is looked up again), the search itself (B, I1), downloading and the audio check (D1.1–D1.3, R0–R3, R6, R9), bringing a video in (F), your verdict (G), re-import (H), pauses (I) and replacing by hand (J) stay as they are. Everything in C, the rest of D and all of E is replaced by this page. Turning judge mode on asks the judge once about every track the rules had skipped (Q5).

Advice modeThe judge only says what it would do

The lookup runs by the main page, unchanged. A card under review carries the judge's answer to the one question of V for the candidate showing: keep, not this one, or unsure, with how sure it is and why (X2). Nothing is decided by it.

Q Is the track looked up, and how?

Asked of every file found in the music folders, and again when a lookup starts. The judge replaces A2–A4: it is handed the track and says whether there is anything to look for, and what to search.

V The judge's verdict

One question, asked once per lookup in judge mode (L6) and once per card in advice mode (X2). The words it is given are under K; they are the rule, and a change to them is a change to this page first.

Judge::verdict

V1What it is shown

  • The track: title, artist, album artist, album, year, length, audio format (lossless, or codec and bitrate); and the other tracks the audio check matched to these uploads, named as such.
  • Each upload: title, channel (every name it is listed under; verified; subscribers, upload date and view count when known; "a channel you allowed"), length, resolution.
  • The audio check: fingerprints, as a share of the video; same waveform, as a share of the track; other audio inside the song, in seconds; placed by offset (R2).
  • The picture: the five frames and the picture check (L4.1).

V2What it answers

  • For each upload: its kind (music video, re-upload of one, lyric video or visualiser, live, cover or other version, fan video, still image, different song, other); its source (the artist's channel · the label, circle or publisher · an official partner · a third party · cannot tell); its version (the same recording · another master or mix of it · another version or performance · cannot tell); whether it is the whole song (whole · a cut · interrupted); whether the library should keep it, and the probability (0 to 1) that it should, which is what the advice shows (X2); a note of one sentence.
  • For the track: a verdict, accept (and which upload), reject all, or unsure; a confidence; a reason.

V3Its standard, in short

Drawn from your verdicts (M) and spelt out under K:

  • Who uploaded it comes first: a credited artist's channel, the label or circle that released the track, the composer's channel, or an official partner (the anime, game, film, publisher or brand the song was made for). A fan's upload, an aggregator channel, a re-upload of the real video: not kept, whatever the picture.
  • The same version: the recording the track is, read off the titles' version words, the credited singer, and the waveform.
  • The whole song: a cut or an interrupted video is a judgement for you (L2).
  • The picture must move: a still is never kept, even with a bar or a ticker over it, whoever uploaded it; a lyric video, a moving illustration or a visualiser on an acceptable channel is kept when no music video is shown; live footage only for a live track; animation is never a sign of a fan work.

V4Its confidence

The probability that you would make the same call: 0.95 and above only when nothing speaks against it; 0.6–0.8 when something does; around 0.5 when the evidence is mixed; "unsure" when it cannot tell. Measured, a confidence of 0.85 and above was right 85% of the time or better on every model (M), which is where the gate stands (W1).

V5When it cannot answer

No key, a key the API refuses, no network, a refusal, or an answer that is not the JSON asked for twice running: the lookup is postponed as for a pause (I5), and the pause says why. Nothing falls back to the rules of thumb; the track is tried again when the pause ends. A lookup already past V is finished by what the judge said.

K What the judge is told

The three sets of words, verbatim. Each is followed by the facts of the case as V1 and S2 list them, and the judge answers in the fields of V2. These words are the rule: refining them is a change to this page.

K1 · The query (Q2, Q3)

You help a music-video library find, on YouTube, the official music video of a track in a personal music collection. Artists come from anywhere; tags may be in the original script, romanised or translated. You are given the track's tags. Write the YouTube search query most likely to put the official music video of this exact song, by this artist, at the top of the results. Use the artist's and song's names as they are most commonly written on YouTube (the original script where the tags are romanised or translated and you know the original; the artist's own romanisation where that is how they write it); add "official video", "MV" or the scene's usual term when it helps. A second, different query may be given for when the first finds nothing. Search for every track, including a live recording (the artist's video of that performance is wanted), a remix or an album mix (the original's video may serve), a short one, a tie-in jingle; the only tracks not to search for are those that cannot have a video of their own: an instrumental, off-vocal or karaoke version, and a drama, talk or interview track. A "Sound:" line, where given, is a measurement of the track's audio: speech has long quiet gaps between phrases and little pulse, music has neither. Where the sound is music, a title that mentions talk, MC or an interview is a song's title, not a talk track.
Answer only with the JSON asked for.

Answers: lookup (yes or no) · skip_reason · queries (one or two) · song_known (what it knows of the song, one sentence).

K2 · The pick (S3)

You help a music-video library choose, from YouTube search results, which uploads to download and listen to for a track in a personal music collection. The goal is the track's official video: the music video released for this exact song by the artist, their collective or their label, on their own channel or the label's; for a live track, the artist's video of that performance; for a version in another language, a remix or a mix, the video of that version where there is one, else the original's. Second best: an official lyric video, visualiser or audio upload from the artist's or label's channel. Not wanted: covers, remixes of a non-remix track, sped-up or slowed versions, arrangements, karaoke, instrumentals, reactions, game footage, dance videos, fan lyric videos and translations, compilations, loops, mixes of several songs, trailers, teasers, live and concert footage of a studio track, and re-uploads of a video by anyone but the artist, their label or an official partner. An upload much shorter than the track is a cut; much longer carries extra footage.
Pick the uploads worth downloading, best first, at most 4; several when it is not clear from the listing which is the real one. Pick none when nothing in the results looks like it could be the track's official video.
Answer only with the JSON asked for.

Answers: picks (ids, best first, with a few words each; empty for none) · reason.

K3 · The verdict (V, X2)

You curate a personal library of music videos. For a track from the library (a music file with its tags) you are shown one or more video uploads that an audio check has already found to contain the song. Your task: say whether an upload is the video the library should keep for this track, and if several are shown, which one.

The library wants, for each track, the official video of that track: the music video released for it by the artist, their label or their collective; where none exists, the artist's or label's own lyric video or visualiser. A still picture set to the audio is never wanted. What matters, in this order:

1. Who uploaded it. Acceptable: a channel belonging to one of the track's credited artists (a performer, the producer or composer, a featured artist; a channel listed under several names is shared by all of them); the label, collective or publisher that released the track; an official partner that released a video of the song (the channel of the film, series, game, brand or company the song was made for, uploading a full-length video of it). A small label's channel can look like a promotion channel, uploading many artists' songs over a still picture; it counts as the label when it is listed beside the artist, or the track's release is its own. Not acceptable, whatever the picture: a fan's channel; a channel that re-uploads other people's videos; an aggregator or promotion channel that is not the track's own label; anybody's re-upload of the real music video. A re-upload is not kept even when no better upload is shown. When you cannot tell whose channel it is (a generic name, nothing in the title or picture that ties it to the artist or a label), the upload is not kept outright: say "cannot tell", lean towards not keeping it, and give at most 0.6.

2. The same version. The upload must be the recording the track is. Read the track's title and album for version words (another language's version, album mix, remix, radio edit, short version, live, acoustic, demo, a featured singer) and the upload's title the same way; the two must agree. The audio check helps: "same waveform" says how much of the track is, sample for sample, the recording in the upload. 90% and above is the same recording; 60–90% is another master or mix of the same performance, or the same backing with another voice; under 50% is another performance (a cover, a live take, a remake). The singer settles the middle band: when the upload credits the track's own artist as the singer it is the track's version even where the waveform is low (another master, a re-recording); when it credits another voice than the track's (a different singer, a synthesised voice where the track is sung by a person or the other way round, a group cover) it is another version even at 60–70%, unless the track's own title or album names that voice. "Fingerprints" say how much of the two is recognisably the same song at all. A title in another script or language is the same song, not another version: the audio check has already settled that it is the song. A track that is itself a remix, an extended mix, an album mix or a re-recording may keep the original's official video when no video of that version exists and the audio check still finds most of the song in it; that is a judgement for a person, so keep it with a middling confidence (0.5–0.7) rather than reject it.

3. The whole song. A video that carries the full song is wanted. A cut (a short version, a broadcast edit, a video well under the track's length) or a video with other audio inside the song (a spoken intro, a scene, an advertisement; "interrupted" in the audio check) is not kept outright: the library may keep it only if nothing fuller exists, and that is a judgement for a person, so give such an upload a lower confidence and say what it is. The track's own length is given; a video more than about 10% shorter is a cut.

4. The picture must move. One picture that never changes for the whole song is not kept, whoever uploaded it, even with a spectrum bar, a progress bar, a ticker or a watermark laid over it: that is a still, and the library does not keep stills. Any real change to the picture itself is enough: the words of the song appearing in it, an illustration that moves, pans, zooms or glows, a visualiser drawn into the picture, motion graphics, several pictures in turn. The picture check settles it: it says how much of the picture ever changes between the frames sampled across the video. Under about 3%, with the frames the same apart from a bar, a ticker or a watermark along an edge, it is a still; where words of the song appear in the frames it is a lyric video, and moves; 10% or more is a moving picture whatever the frames look like at a glance, and calling it a still against the check is wrong. On an acceptable channel a moving illustration, a lyric video or a visualiser is kept when no music video is shown.

Where several acceptable uploads are shown, prefer: the music video over a lyric video or visualiser; the track's own artist's channel over a collaborator's; the recording that matches the track's version most closely; the full-length one over a cut.

Decide from the title, the channel, the lengths, the audio check and the frames together. Be decisive. Give confidence as the probability that a careful person curating this library would make the same call: 0.95 and above only when nothing speaks against it; 0.6–0.8 when something does (a cut, a version question, a channel that may or may not be the label's); around 0.5 when the evidence is mixed; "unsure" as the verdict only when you cannot tell at all.
Answer only with the JSON asked for.

Answers: for each upload: index · kind · source · version · whole · keep · confidence · note. For the track: verdict (accept, reject all, unsure) · chosen · confidence · reason.

The search (K1, K2)

Whether the judge's own query and pick find the upload that ended up kept. Measured on tracks whose video you accepted by hand, on tracks the rules took outright, on tracks you rejected every option of, and on tracks the rules found nothing for. The search was run as a guest at first; YouTube answers a guest it has had enough of with an empty list (S1), which is what stopped that attempt after a hundred queries, and the rest was run signed in. The first wording of K1 declined to search for live tracks, remixes and a tie-in jingle, all of which you had kept videos for, and the first K2 picked the Japanese music video over the English version's for an English-version track; the adopted wording searches for everything but instrumentals and talk, and asks for the track's own version first. The tracks the rules found nothing for are where the judge may find videos the rules could not; whether its picks are right there is not known until they are listened to and judged (V).
