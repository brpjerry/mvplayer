MV Player import rules: the judge

How a track comes to have a music video when a model is asked instead of the rules of thumb. Two things the judge can do, chosen in Settings → Judge. In judge mode it runs the lookup: it writes the search, picks what is listened to and gives the verdict on every upload; what the main page drops on sight, whose channel an upload is on, version and fan words, small channels and the score no longer apply, and what stays are the audio check, the length check and your verdict on what the judge is not sure of. In advice mode nothing in the lookup changes: each card under review shows what the judge would do and how sure it is. Rules of the main page are cited by their ids (R0, G1); this page's own are Q1, S2 …

A new page, proposed: not approved yet, and nothing in the code implements it. The words the judge is given (K) were refined against $n_lab of your own verdicts and measured on three models (M); the numbers under N follow from that.

The two modes

Judge modeThe judge runs the lookup

Q → S → L → V → W, then your verdict (X) on what it leaves. Of the main page, A1 and A6–A11 (when a track is looked up again), the search itself (B, I1), downloading and the audio check (D1.1–D1.3, R0–R3, R6, R9), bringing a video in (F), your verdict (G), re-import (H), pauses (I) and replacing by hand (J) stay as they are. Everything in C, the rest of D and all of E is replaced by this page.

Advice modeThe judge only says what it would do

The lookup runs by the main page, unchanged. A card under review carries the judge's answer to the one question of V for the candidate showing: keep, not this one, or unsure, with how sure it is and why (X2). Nothing is decided by it.

Q Is the track looked up, and how?

Asked of every file found in the music folders, and again when a lookup starts. The judge replaces A2–A4: it is handed the track and says whether there is anything to look for, and what to search.

    • Q2The judge is handed the track's tags and says there is nothing to look forskipped, with the judge's reason It is given the title, artist, album artist, album, year, length, audio format, genre and the folder the file is in, and the words under K. An instrumental, off-vocal or karaoke version, a talk track, a jingle, a skit: it says so in a few words, and the track is skipped as A2–A4 skipped it. Those three rules no longer apply; what they said (1:20, the audio-only and talk words) is in the judge's words as guidance. A skipped track is looked up again when the words change (A9).

The second query is asked only when the first gives nothing the judge would listen to (S3).

When a track is looked up again, A6–A11 hold as they stand: no video after 14 days, a failed lookup after 30 minutes doubling, a re-import at once (H). The judge's answers are kept a day with the search results, so a lookup repeated within the day asks nothing twice.

S Search and the pick

The queries are asked as on the main page (B): ten results each, as a guest (I1), remembered for a day. What replaces C: nothing is dropped on sight, there is no score, and the judge picks what is listened to from the listing.

L Listening: what stays

Each pick, in the judge's order: its audio is downloaded and laid against the track as today (D1.1–D1.3, I3), and the audio check measures what it measures (R0–R3, R6, R9). The rules of thumb of D are gone; what is left are gates. A gate never takes a video outright and never throws one out that the judge would keep: it decides between outright and your verdict.

V The judge's verdict

One question, asked once per lookup in judge mode (L6) and once per card in advice mode (X2). The words it is given are under K; they are the rule, and a change to them is a change to this page first.

Judge::verdict

V1What it is shown

  • The track: title, artist, album artist, album, year, length, audio format (lossless, or codec and bitrate); and the other tracks the audio check matched to these uploads, named as such.
  • Each upload: title, channel (every name it is listed under; verified; subscribers, upload date and view count when known; "a channel you allowed"), length, resolution.
  • The audio check: fingerprints, as a share of the video; same waveform, as a share of the track; other audio inside the song, in seconds; placed by offset (R2).
  • The picture: the five frames and the picture check (L4.1).

V2What it answers

  • For each upload: its kind (music video, re-upload of one, lyric video or visualiser, live, cover or other version, fan video, still image, different song, other); its source (the artist's channel · the label, circle or publisher · an official partner · a third party · cannot tell); its version (the same recording · another master or mix of it · another version or performance · cannot tell); whether it is the whole song (whole · a cut · interrupted); whether the library should keep it, and the probability (0 to 1) that it should, which is what the advice shows (X2); a note of one sentence.
  • For the track: a verdict, accept (and which upload), reject all, or unsure; a confidence; a reason.

V3Its standard, in short

Drawn from your verdicts (M) and spelt out under K:

  • Who uploaded it comes first: a credited artist's channel, the label or circle that released the track, the composer's channel, or an official partner (the anime, game, film, publisher or brand the song was made for). A fan's upload, an aggregator channel, a re-upload of the real video: not kept, whatever the picture.
  • The same version: the recording the track is, read off the titles' version words, the credited singer, and the waveform.
  • The whole song: a cut or an interrupted video is a judgement for you (L2).
  • The picture must move: a still is never kept, even with a bar or a ticker over it, whoever uploaded it; a lyric video, a moving illustration or a visualiser on an acceptable channel is kept when no music video is shown; live footage only for a live track; animation is never a sign of a fan work.

V4Its confidence

The probability that you would make the same call: 0.95 and above only when nothing speaks against it; 0.6–0.8 when something does; around 0.5 when the evidence is mixed; "unsure" when it cannot tell. Measured, a confidence of $gate and above was right $gate_pct of the time or better on every model (M), which is where the gate stands (W1).

V5When it cannot answer

No key, a key the API refuses, no network, a refusal, or an answer that is not the JSON asked for twice running: the lookup is postponed as for a pause (I5), and the pause says why. Nothing falls back to the rules of thumb; the track is tried again when the pause ends. A lookup already past V is finished by what the judge said.

A lookup takes minutes, and another one may settle the videos a track is a candidate of in the meantime. What the track is a candidate of is read again before its result is written, as on the main page (E).

X Your verdict, and the advice

Your verdict is what it is on the main page (G1–G10): accept, reject, allow or deny a channel, undo. The judge has no standing on a card once it is yours.

ImportManager::approveVideo · rejectVideo · Judge::advise · ReviewPanel.qml
  • X1Your verdict G stands, every rule of it. Allowing a channel (G7) judges its videos under review again by the main page's R1 in rules mode; in judge mode they are not judged again, since the judge weighed the channel already, but the judge is told of the channel from then on (S2, V1). Denying one (G8) is as it was in either mode.
  • X2The advice on a card For the candidate showing (G3): "Judge: keep · 92%", "Judge: not this one · 85%", or "Judge: unsure", in the review panel under the candidate's name, with its note, source, version and whole-song answer when the pointer rests on it. In judge mode it is the verdict already given (V2) for that upload and candidate. In advice mode it is asked when the card first shows, for the candidate showing with the other candidates named, from the video's own file and what the library kept of the upload: nothing is fetched from YouTube. Asked once per video and candidate, and kept in the library; "Ask again" is in the panel for when the words or the model change. Advice mode changes nothing else: the lookup runs by the main page.
  • X3What is logged Every answer of the judge, as an event judge in the import log: the question (query, pick or verdict), the model, the track and the uploads, the answer, tokens and cost. Your verdict on a card that carried advice is logged beside it. That is what the next measurement (M) reads: your verdicts against its advice, so that the words under K can be refined again.
  • X4Settings → Judge The key, as it is. The mode: off, advice, judge. The model: Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5, each with what a lookup costs (M); Sonnet 5.5 by default. In judge mode the gate is fixed (N) and the tab says how many lookups the judge has decided alone and how many it left for you. Switching modes changes nothing in the library: a lookup already under way finishes as it began, and the next starts in the new mode.

M Measured

Against your own verdicts. On 8 October 2026 the review queue was frozen as judge input (594 videos, their frames and the audio check's figures), before your hand review of it; by 11 October you had decided $n_lab of those groups: $n_acc accepted, $n_rej with every option rejected, $n_part in part ($n_opt uploads in all). The judge was asked the verdict question (K3) about each group with five frames per upload, and its answers read against yours. The words went through four versions, each refined on what the one before got wrong, on all three models; a fifth then took out every named channel, artist and scene-specific word list, so that the words hold for any library and not only the part of this one they were refined on: the table shows what the generality cost. A sixth rules out stills (L4) and is given the picture check (L4.1); a seventh, the wording adopted, makes the check decisive, after the sixth had called moving pictures stills on the strength of the frames alone (one changed 62% of the picture, another 98%). The verdict (K3)

"Decided alone" is what judge mode would have settled without you: a confidence of at least $gate, the chosen upload the whole song (L2). "Agrees with you" counts every group, including those it would have left for your verdict.

$measured

What the disagreements were, once the words were refined: cuts and interrupted videos from the artist's own channel, which you kept for some tracks and turned down for others (hence L2); a netlabel's lyric video or moving illustration with a spectrum bar that you kept (Firefly Music, Diversity), which the judge cannot tell from an aggregator's unless told (hence the netlabel line in K3, and allowing the channel, G7); the Vocaloid original on the producer's channel for a track that is a singer's cover of it, which you turned down (hence the singer line in K3); a few re-uploads of a real music video on a nondescript channel, which you turned down (hence "cannot tell" is never kept outright); and a track that is a remix or an extended mix, for which you kept the original's video (hence the remix line in K3: it gained Opus four groups and cost Haiku a few, which it now calls unsure). The adopted wording keeps each of these as a principle (who uploaded it, the credited singer, a small label's channel, a remix track) without the names that taught it.

The search (K1, K2)

Whether the judge's own query and pick find the upload that ended up kept. Measured on tracks whose video you accepted by hand, on tracks the rules took outright, on tracks you rejected every option of, and on tracks the rules found nothing for. The search was run as a guest at first; YouTube answers a guest it has had enough of with an empty list (S1), which is what stopped that attempt after a hundred queries, and the rest was run signed in. The first wording of K1 declined to search for live tracks, remixes and a tie-in jingle, all of which you had kept videos for, and the first K2 picked the Japanese music video over the English version's for an English-version track; the adopted wording searches for everything but instrumentals and talk, and asks for the track's own version first. The tracks the rules found nothing for are where the judge may find videos the rules could not; whether its picks are right there is not known until they are listened to and judged (V).
